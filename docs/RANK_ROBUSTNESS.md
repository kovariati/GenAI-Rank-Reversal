# Rank weighting and scale sensitivity\n\nThis technical note provides the machine-readable implementation context and extended outputs for the composition/scale-sensitivity formalism presented in Appendix A of the associated article. It is a sensitivity operation, not a recommendation to pool distinct estimands.\n\nLet the direct active-arm contrast under the assisted design be \(D_A\), and under the independent design be \(D_I\). For a descriptive scalarization, define\n\n\[\nD(w,\lambda)=w\lambda D_A+(1-w)D_I,\n\]\n\nwhere \(w\in[0,1]\) is the declared weight on the assisted design and \(\lambda>0\) is a positive relative-scale convention. When \(D_A>0\) and \(D_I<0\), the tie boundary is\n\n\[\nw^*(\lambda)=\frac{-D_I}{\lambda D_A-D_I}.\n\]\n\nIf admissible assumptions are \(w\in[w_L,w_U]\) and \(\lambda\in[\lambda_L,\lambda_U]\), the resulting scalar ordering can be classified as robustly positive, robustly negative, or assumption-sensitive/tie-compatible by comparing the admissible weight interval with the tie boundary across the allowed scale range.\n\nThe analysis makes one point explicit: once two construct-linked assessment designs give opposite intervention orderings, a single regime-agnostic scalar ranking requires additional weighting and relative-scale assumptions. The randomized design-specific contrasts do not identify those choices.\n\n## Executable artifacts\n\n- `code/assessment_regime_rank_robustness.py`\n- `code/assessment_regime_rank_sensitivity.py`\n- `code/derive_wong_regime_mixture.py`\n- `results/wong_rank_robustness_region.csv`\n- `results/wong_rank_robustness_summary.csv`\n- `results/wong_scale_composition_sensitivity.csv`\n- `results/wong_scale_composition_sensitivity_keypoints.csv`\n- `results/wong_regime_mixture_threshold.csv`\n\nThe numerical location of a tie boundary is convention dependent. Strict opposite-sign reversal itself is a separate claim and does not depend on this scalarization.\n
